## Introduction

Medals are more than just pieces of metal—they are symbols of achievement, recognition, and excellence. Designing a medal requires balancing aesthetic appeal, clarity of symbolism, and functional durability.

When I first approached medal design, I realized the task involved both art and strategy. Every detail, from size and shape to texture and color, communicates meaning.

## Context of My Medal Design

I submitted my design for the Lausanne 2020 Youth Olympic Games Medal Design Competition ([lausanne.medal-design-competition.com](https://lausanne.medal-design-competition.com/)).

The theme was **“Celebrating the Spirit of the Winter Olympic Games.”** I aimed to capture athletic excellence, winter beauty, and global unity in a single piece.

Out of 200–300 entries worldwide, my design was one of only 9 submissions from India. This made the experience especially meaningful and memorable.

## Conceptualization

The design process starts with understanding the purpose:

- **Event or Competition**: Tailoring design elements to the theme or sport.
- **Symbolism**: Incorporating meaningful icons, motifs, or text.
- **Recipient Experience**: Ensuring the medal feels valuable and memorable.

Sketching multiple concepts is essential. Often, the first few ideas are exploratory; refinement emerges through iteration and feedback.

## Design Elements

1. **Shape and Form**: Circular, hexagonal, or custom silhouettes can convey different moods.
2. **Relief and Texture**: Embossed or engraved details add depth and tactility.
3. **Material and Finish**: Choice of metal, plating, or patina affects both aesthetics and perception of value.
4. **Color Accents**: Enamel, paint, or other materials highlight key design features.
5. **Typography**: Clear, readable text reinforces the medal’s significance.

## Iteration and Prototyping

- Creating digital mockups allows testing proportions, spacing, and visual hierarchy.
- 3D models or clay prototypes help visualize how the medal will feel in hand.
- Feedback from event organizers or potential recipients can uncover overlooked improvements.

## Key Takeaways

- A successful medal design balances elegance, clarity, and symbolism.
- Attention to small details—edge finishing, texture, engraving depth—elevates the final product.
- Iterative refinement ensures the medal is not only visually appealing but also meaningful to recipients.
- Participating in competitions provides unique opportunities to learn, grow, and share your vision with a global audience.

## Conclusion

Creating this medal was a deeply rewarding experience. I hope you enjoy viewing it as much as I enjoyed designing it. Medal design is a fusion of creativity, precision, and empathy. The goal is to craft a piece that celebrates achievement, tells a story, and endures as a treasured keepsake.
